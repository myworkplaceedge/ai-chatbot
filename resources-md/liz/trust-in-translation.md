# 1. Lesson Overview

**Lesson Title:** Trust in Translation

**Pathway:** We

**Skill Focus:** Repairing misunderstandings and creating clarity from confusion

**Workplace Challenge:** This lesson helps learners repair inevitable misunderstandings with warmth and clarity, and without placing blame or becoming defensive.

Synthesia Video/ 360 Content **Why It Matters: **Even the strongest communicators can sometimes feel misunderstood, regardless of whether they are using the "right" words. Subtle differences in tone, pacing, or non-verbal cues can shift the meaning of a message, and differences in cultural norms can vary the interpretation of those behaviours. When misunderstandings happen, many people either double down to avoid looking incompetent or withdraw to avoid causing offence. This gets in the way of effective and wholehearted communication.

Synthesia Video/ 360 Content

**Learning Objectives**

Learners will be able to:

- Perceive when their message has not landed clearly.
- Use repair language to clarify their meaning and strengthen the relationship after a misunderstanding.
- Monitor and adjust tone and body language to support verbal clarity.

Narrator: Welcome to this episode of *Missed Meanings, *the podcast where miscommunication turns into opportunity!

Misunderstandings don’t always happen because someone used the wrong words. In global teams where tone, body language, and expectations can differ, miscommunication often shows up in subtle ways.

In the next few minutes, you’ll hear Cemre and Tomas working together on a shared project. Over the course of three short interactions, you’ll hear how small shifts in delivery (their tone, pacing, or word choice) can change the outcome of the conversation.

The first version of each interaction demonstrates a misunderstanding. The second version shows how the same message can be repaired to maintain clarity, connection, and respect.

Interaction 1: Asking for Clarification

Cemre:

Hmm. These numbers seem off compared to last week.

Tomas:

Okay, well, I did the analysis the same way I always do.

Cemre:

Oh, no! I wasn’t questioning your work, I just meant… never mind.

**Narrator: **Cemre’s intention was to flag a discrepancy. But Tomas heard it as criticism. Why? Cemre’s tone was flat and direct, and Tomas, already under pressure, interpreted her words as a challenge.

Neither Cemre nor Tomas is to blame for the misunderstanding. It’s about a gap between what was meant and what was heard.

What could Cemre have said that would have communicated her point more clearly? Let’s take a look.

Cemre:

Hey Tomas, I noticed the numbers look a bit different from last week. I might be missing something. Could we take a look together?

Tomas:

Sure, thanks for catching that. Let me pull it up.

**Narrator: **This time, Cemre shifts her tone and pacing. She softens the entry with, “I might be missing something,” which invites collaboration rather than defensiveness.

Interaction 2: Feedback that Feels Like Criticism

Tomas:

It seems like this part doesn’t really work. It’s kind of off-brand for us.

Cemre:

Oh. Okay…I mean, I thought I was following the brief.

**Narrator: **Tomas notices Cemre pulling back. Instead of ignoring it or starting over, he offers a repair: softening his tone, acknowledging her effort, and clarifying his intention. This keeps the conversation collaborative and constructive.

Tomas:

**Narrator: **Cemre didn’t say anything directly, but Tomas picked up on her shift. Instead of letting the tension linger, he made space for a delayed repair. # <a id="_heading=h.1jhuiexkqxle"></a>Glossary: 360 content, separate lesson?

**Tone, noun**: The general attitude, feeling, or character expressed in speech, writing, or behavior. It can influence how a message is received or interpreted.
Example:* He adjusted the ****tone**** of his presentation to be more collaborative when speaking with the executive team.*

**To get (one’s) wires crossed, idiom - verb phrase**: To have a misunderstanding, especially when two people think they are talking about the same thing but are not.**
**Example: We must have **gotten our wires crossed**! I thought the meeting was at 2:00 p.m., not 3:00 p.m.

​​**To land, verb - metaphor**: To be received, understood, or interpreted in a certain way by others, often referring to how a message, idea, or action is perceived.**
**Example: *His joke didn’t ****land**** the way he hoped. It came across as insensitive rather than funny.*

**To clear the air, idiom - verb phrase**: To have an open and honest conversation in order to resolve tension, misunderstandings, or conflict.**
**Example: *After the disagreement, they scheduled a meeting ****to clear the air**** and rebuild trust within the team.*

A small check-in like, “Can I clarify something?” is powerful. It acknowledges the relationship without assigning blame. That’s how trust grows, one small moment at a time.

Step 2: Reflect

As you can see, miscommunication doesn’t always happen because someone has made a mistake or said something wrong. When people miscommunicate in multi-cultural teams, it is often simply due to differing norms and expectations in communication.

Repair language helps you reconnect when your message didn’t land* as intended. It does *not* mean you’re admitting fault or assigning blame; it means you’re creating space for clarity.

You heard a few great examples of repair language in Cemre and Tomas’s conversation. Let’s have a look again.

Tomas: Hey, can I clarify something? When I said, “If you think that’s the best use of time,” I wasn’t trying to question your judgment. I trust your instinct on what the client wants. I just didn’t say it clearly.

Cemre: Oh, thanks! I wasn’t sure, honestly. It sounded a bit like you were doubting me. I’m glad you said something.

In these examples, Cemre and Tomas are working together to clear the air* between them. Without this key step, they may begin to feel resentment towards one another, avoid working on projects together, or even begin to dislike one another. Using repair language when there’s been a miscommunication allows them to continue working closely, successfully, and happily.

Here are some more great ways to check in with your conversation partner when you think you’ve gotten your wires crossed*.

- “Let me rephrase that; I think I wasn’t clear.”
- “Can I try that again? I want to make sure I expressed myself correctly.”
- “That didn’t come out quite right. Here’s what I meant.”
- “I think we might be hearing this differently. Can we pause and reset?”
- “I’m not sure we’re on the same page. Can you tell me what you heard?”

These phrases take ownership of the moment without assigning blame.

Step 3: Evaluate

# <a id="_heading=h.1jhuiexkqxle"></a>Glossary:

**Tone, noun**: The general attitude, feeling, or character expressed in speech, writing, or behavior. It can influence how a message is received or interpreted.
Example:* He adjusted the ****tone**** of his presentation to be more collaborative when speaking with the executive team.*

**To get (one’s) wires crossed, idiom - verb phrase**: To have a misunderstanding, especially when two people think they are talking about the same thing but are not.**
**Example: We must have **gotten our wires crossed**! I thought the meeting was at 2:00 p.m., not 3:00 p.m.

​​**To land, verb - metaphor**: To be received, understood, or interpreted in a certain way by others, often referring to how a message, idea, or action is perceived.**
**Example: *His joke didn’t ****land**** the way he hoped. It came across as insensitive rather than funny.*

**To clear the air, idiom - verb phrase**: To have an open and honest conversation in order to resolve tension, misunderstandings, or conflict.**
**Example: *After the disagreement, they scheduled a meeting ****to clear the air**** and rebuild trust within the team.*
