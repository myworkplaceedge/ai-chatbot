### Emotions are Data - summary.pdf

Summary

                            Understanding Emotional Signals at Work
   Why this matters:
   Emotions are not the problem, they are information

      Emotions can influence:                       By interpreting emotions we can:
        communication                                   have better judgement
        decision-making                                 greater self-control
        collaboration                                   stronger relationships
        conflict resolution                             improved accountability
        professional reputation

                The Brain’s Three Layers                                Stress vs Pressure
  1. Brain Stem (“Reptile Brain”)
                                                                   Stress:
       controls survival function (breathing, heart rate)
       activates flight, fight or freeze
                                                                       internal
                                                                       fears, doubts, insecurity
  2. Limbic System (“Emotional Brain”)                                 unique to the individual
       process emotion
       forms emotional memories                                    Pressure:
       includes the amygdala
                                                                       external
                                                                       deadlines, expectations,
  3. Neo Cortex (“Thinking Brain”)
      reasoning and analysis                                           performance demands
      planning and decision-making                                     often shared across
      language and impulse control                                     teams

  Amygdala hijack: when the amygdala perceives danger,
  it can override logical thinking.

                                                                 Negativity Bias
                                                    Our brains are wired to:
                                                       notice criticism faster than praise
                                                       assume threat before safety
                                                       prepare for worse-case outcomes

                                                    Bias that once supported survival can now
                                                    lead to:
                                                       catastrophizing
                                                       misreading tone
                                                       overreacting to feedback
                                                       assuming negative intent
Summary

                       Reacting vs Responding
  Reacting may look like:             Responding may look like:
     sending a tense email               pausing before speaking
     interrupting                        naming the emotion
     shutting down                       checking assumptions
     raising your voice                  asking clarifying questions
     defensive posture                   choosing professional language

                                    Emotional Signals Are Not Universal
                                   Includes:
                                       direct eye contract
                                       silence
                                       loud enthusiasm
                                       reserved tone

                                   Note: These may signal different emotions
                                   depending on cultural norms

     Responsibility vs Accountability
  Responsibility
     assigned duties
     can be delegated
     appears in job descriptions
  Accountability
     owning outcomes
     can’t be delegated
     may require emotional maturity
     often occurs after action

                       Instead of Flight, Fight or Freeze
             Practice: Regroup, Reflect and Respond
